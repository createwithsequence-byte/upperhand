01 The read in one breath

A working firm, presented by a template.

Pedersen & Houpt is a sixty-nine-year-old Chicago business firm with twenty-six attorneys, a Chambers listing this year and deals like the Thompson Center on its record. Its website is a vendor template whose current build dates from May 2021 and whose words date from 2012, with stock photographs of skydivers and hard hats, a consent wall across the first screen, and an About page that still says the firm is fifty-five years old. The content underneath is better than the site that carries it.

02 What a client sees in the first five seconds

Skydivers, a category headline, and a wall.

  • The headline is a category, not the firm. "Grow Your Business" could sit on a bank, a software company or a gym. Nothing on the first screen says middle market, Chicago, 1957, or law.
  • The photography is a metaphor. Skydivers holding hands, two men in hard hats, a finger on a glowing network. Not one photograph of a person who works at 161 North Clark.
  • The consent bar takes the fold. 140 of 900 pixels on desktop, about two fifths of a phone screen, on every page and every visit until it is accepted. It covers the firm's own description of itself.

04 Twenty-six attorneys, twenty-seven bio pages · the pages a client actually reads

The best content on the site is under the wall.

  • The index shows no one. A visitor picking a lawyer sees letters A to Z, a title filter and a practice list. Twenty-six real, consistent headshots exist one click deeper.
  • The bio content is strong. Eric Kordish's page carries fifteen representative matters with dollar figures and the Chambers Spotlight 2026 badge. It is the most persuasive writing on the site and it sits below a gray band, a tab strip and the bar.
  • Each bio weighs about 1 MB and takes 1.1 seconds to load on a fast connection; on a phone the photograph arrives, then the wall.
05 Copy is design · what the pages say

The About page is fourteen years old.

Read on the page

"This combination serves the firm as well today as it did when it was first founded more than fifty-five years ago." The firm was founded in 1957. Fifty-five years ago was 2012. It has been sixty-nine.

The press link

The only press item linked from the site is Crains.pdf: Crain's Chicago Business, 23 January 2013, the obituary of founder Peer Pedersen. Respectful, and thirteen years old.

The title tag

"Pedersen & Houpt: Elite Chicago Law Firm" is what Google shows as the headline for the homepage. The firm does not use the word elite anywhere else.

The services pages are competent and brochure-like: "superlative service and responsive representation." The bios, by contrast, are specific. The rebuild should sound like the bios.

07 Phone pass · 375 wide, the same pages

It fits a phone. It was not made for one.

  • Nothing overflows, which is the good news: the template collapses to one column on all fifteen pages we measured.
  • Tap targets fail Google's size check on the homepage (eight flagged) and every page has between 14 and 38 links under 40 pixels.
  • The phone menu is iOS 6. Two gray beveled buttons, a magnifier and the word MENU. The bar under it says whatever page you are on, in the same gray.

12 The keep list · what a rebuild carries forward

Four things on this site are already right.

The wordmark

"Pedersen" with the red rule

A real mark: a serif name, a single red line, the full name beneath. It has held since 2012 and it should anchor the rebuild. The red, #AB1E23, is the accent this deck wears.

The bios

Representative experience

Dollar figures, deal shapes, industries. Fifteen matters on one partner's page. This is the proof clients look for and it becomes the front of the new site, not the bottom of a tab.

The headshots

One standard, twenty-six times

Same backdrop, same crop, same light. A rare discipline on a firm site. They belong on the index, at size, with names and practices.

The positioning, hidden in the nav

We Listen · It's About Results · Alternative Fee Arrangements

Three pages under "Client Focus" say exactly what a middle-market owner wants to hear. Today they are a sidebar. They are the homepage.

Also kept: the Chambers Spotlight 2026 badge, the client Payment and EACCESS links in the footer (moved up front), and the 2020 alert cadence as proof the firm can publish fast when it matters.

13 Design verdict · our house standard, the same scale we hold our own work to

A four, with a seven inside it.

Cold read, three seconds

Corporate template, 2012 vintage, rebuilt in 2021. Skydivers over a law firm. The one word the site evokes is brochure. A client cannot say from the first screen who the firm is for or why it is different.

The score · 4 of 10

Template. Nothing is wrong by rule and nothing was chosen: the vendor's layout, stock metaphors, a category headline, default type. Above broken because it works, is consistent and is honest. Below professional because the copy is 2012, the map is blank and the first interaction is a consent wall.

What raises it

The seven trapped inside is the bio content and the wordmark. Put the people first, write the homepage in the voice of the bios, and replace metaphor photography with the firm itself, and this site reaches signature territory without changing what the firm says about itself.

The ten-out-of-ten version of this exact site: a Chicago firm that looks like the room it works in. The homepage says in one sentence who it serves and shows the people who do the work, at 161 North Clark, in their own light. The bios read on a phone in the elevator on the way up to the meeting. The news reads as counsel, with a face beside it.

14 What changes for a client · each tied to a finding

Six things a client would notice.

Slide 02

The first screen says who you are for

One sentence about middle-market owners and their companies, the firm's own people in the photograph, proof beside it: Chambers, 1957, the Thompson Center.

Slide 04

Faces on the index, matters up front

Twenty-six attorneys by face, practice and one line. The bio opens with representative experience, not a tab strip.

Slide 09

Loads in under two seconds on a phone

Right-sized images in modern formats, nothing blocking the first paint. From 2.8 to 7.8 seconds today to under 2.5 is a build default, not an add-on.

Slide 02 · 07

No wall, real buttons

Consent handled in a slim bar or not at all, given what the site actually tracks. Every link big enough to tap.

Slide 10

Google shows the firm's own words

A description on every page, one title per page, one H1, a sitemap with no dead ends. Search results that read like the firm wrote them.

Slide 06

News with a face and a follow

Each item carries its author's photograph and a practice tag. A visitor can follow one practice. The 2020 speed becomes the normal pace.

What this deck does not do: propose a scope, a timeline or a price. Those come after the firm decides what it wants the site to be. This is the case that it should be something.
