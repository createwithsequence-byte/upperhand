02 Overview · sources dated 9 Sep 2026 unless noted

A middle-market firm that says so only in directories.

Entity and office

Pedersen & Houpt, P.C.

"A Professional Corporation" in the footer and in the 2013 ISBA notice; "P.C." in case captions. One office at 161 N. Clark, Suite 2700 (an older FindLaw listing says Suite 3000, so a floor move, unverified). Illinois Secretary of State filing could not be pulled: the state's site blocks scripted requests.

The live site says "large publicly-held entities and middle-market companies to small businesses, start-up companies, and individuals." The sharper line, "middle market, early stage and start-up companies and their owners… real estate and multi-unit retail developers on a national basis," exists only in the FindLaw and Lawyers.com copy.

Peer Pedersen (1925 to 2013) was "known more for his deal-making than his law practice": part of the 1990s group behind Waste Management, Boston Chicken and Blockbuster; Lyric Opera board; a $1M gift in memory of his wife Sarah in 2008. That is the only press item the site links, as Crains.pdf.

12 What the site says · what the record shows

The copy promises immersion. The record shows a firm that stopped narrating.

focus-results.html

"All members of our firm share a single goal: to achieve client objectives while exceeding their expectations."

about.html

"Pedersen & Houpt lawyers become immersed in their clients' businesses. We get to know the people, culture, business, and industry in which our clients work and operate."

homepage hero

"Provide Enhanced Solutions." "Pull Together to Gain Momentum." "The Right Connections to Build Solutions." "Grow Your Business."

  • Immersion is real and undated. Fifteen matters on one bio, the Thompson Center, Skolnik, a five-week trade-secret trial. None with a year on the results page.
  • Nobody speaks. Zero attributed quotes in the firm's own announcements or the trade press since 2024. The site's voice is the vendor's.
  • Momentum is invisible. Four hires and a first Chambers ranking in two years, seven names gone without a word, one memorial. The homepage shows skydivers.
  • The client tools exist. LawPay and EACCESS links live in the footer. A client has to know to look.

The gap is not ambition. It is narration. The firm has a story since 2013 and has not told it anywhere a client looks.

15 Constraints · what the decision sits inside

Small firm, partner-heavy, a quote already on the table.

Budget

A 26-lawyer P.C. with an estimated $10 to 25M in revenue and nineteen partners who each see the bill. Another firm has quoted a five-year package; Anthony's word for it: "NUTS." The number is the objection, not the idea.

Scope

One office, seven groups, 26 bios, 34 service pages, 83 newsroom pages, a brochure PDF, LawPay, EACCESS, a newsletter with a broken sign-up. Everything is small. The content problem is dates and voice, not volume.

Skin in the game

Chambers ranked the real estate group for the first time in August 2026 and the site shows it in 15px on the third screen. Four juniors hired since 2024 are recruiting on a site that shows skydivers.

Calendar

Year-end is planning season for a P.C.; the 2014 build-out ran on a hard date. Chambers submissions and Super Lawyers cycles run in Q1. A site that can carry the next ranking is worth more before it than after.

What they have shipped

2012: a real redesign after Flash. 2014: a new office on an aggressive timeline. 2020: fifteen alerts in a year. 2021: a template refresh. 2024 to 25: four hires. 2026: a first Chambers ranking, one memorial page done with care.

What they have not

Photographed themselves. Put a year on their proof. Claimed their Google profile. Written a sentence that says who the firm is for. Spoken in public since 2024. Gaps, not preferences; the website is the one place all five can be fixed at once.

16 Do · Don't · Openers that fit the record

Lead with the audit, not the agency.

Do
  • Send the audit deck through Anthony so it arrives as a partner's find, not a vendor's pitch.
  • Pitch Collins in the words the partners used to elect him: growing "the Firm, its client base, and markets," "exceptional value and client service," "attract top talent." Then a term sheet: what, who, how long, what happens if it slips.
  • Use their own vocabulary: "We Listen," "It's About Results," "Alternative Fee Arrangements." Those pages are the homepage they have not built.
  • Give Muehlstein the history. A timeline from 1957 with Peer's deals in it, done properly, is the veto turned into the sponsor.
  • Bring a photographer, not a mood board. The one asset they cannot download.
Don't
  • Don't quote against the other firm's number in the first meeting. Win on the case, then price.
  • Don't say template, lame or dated to anyone but Anthony. Partners approved this site twice.
  • Don't propose a five-year retainer. That is the exact shape that made Anthony write "NUTS."
  • Don't lead with technology. Nobody there has said a public word about it. Lead with the client in the elevator.
  • Don't touch the wordmark. It is the one designed thing they own.
Openers that fit the record
  • "Your About page says fifty-five years. You are sixty-nine." Cites about.html; every partner will check it on the spot.
  • "Chambers ranked you for the first time in August. It is on your third screen in 15-pixel type." Cites the Aug 2026 news item and the homepage.
  • "Burke Warren was on your exact template in January 2022 and got a new one by January 2023, from your own vendor." Cites the Wayback captures. For Muehlstein: "You cut the rent thirty percent in 2014. This costs less than a year of the difference." Cites Crain's, May 2015.
  • "Eric Kordish's page has fifteen deals with dollar figures on it. That is the best writing on your site and it is under a cookie wall." Cites the bio.

17 Four openings · labeled as the author's read

01 · The rebuild, bought the 2014 way

Fixed scope, a hard date, a small group, the COO

What: the site the audit describes: people first, dated proof, no wall, fast on a phone. Why: the office, the firm's one purchase of this size, was bought this way (slide 14); Anthony's objection is the other firm's shape, not the idea. Frame it as a pilot: homepage and ten bios live by a date, or no second phase.

02 · The photo day

One day at 161 North Clark, twenty-six people

What: a shoot that produces the index faces, the homepage and a year of news imagery. Why: no photograph of a person at the firm exists on the site in any era, and it is the one thing the incumbent vendor cannot template. Sellable alone; it makes the rebuild inevitable.

03 · Claim the record

Google profile, directories, dated results

What: claim and fix the Google Business Profile (2.8 stars, "Own this business?"), correct Suite 3100 in FindLaw and Lexology, date every matter. Why: cheap, fast, measurable, and where a referral looks before the site (slide 10). A first invoice that earns the second.

04 · Narration as a retainer, monthly not five-year

One item a month with a face on it

What: a monthly cadence that turns hires, rankings, deals and memorials into dated, authored items; the newsletter form fixed. Why: the firm publishes five times a year and has no public voice (slide 12); the 2020 pace proves it can. Month to month, cancelable, the opposite of the quote on the table.

Counterparty read: a 69-year-old P.C. with nineteen partners and no distress signals found. Normal terms. The risk is a committee that cannot decide, not a client that cannot pay. First move: the audit deck, forwarded by Anthony, with one sentence from John.
