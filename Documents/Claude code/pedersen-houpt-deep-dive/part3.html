15 Constraints · what the decision sits inside

Small firm, partner-heavy, a quote already on the table.

Budget

A 26-lawyer P.C. with an estimated $10 to 25M in revenue and nineteen partners who each see the bill. Another firm has quoted a five-year package. Anthony's own words: it "seems NUTS." The number is the objection; the case for doing anything at all is not.

Scope

One office, seven groups, 26 bios, 34 service pages, 83 newsroom pages, a brochure PDF, LawPay, EACCESS, a newsletter with a broken sign-up form. Everything that exists is small. The content problem is dates and voice, not volume.

Skin in the game

Chambers ranked the real estate group for the first time in August 2026 and the site shows it in 15px on the third screen. Four juniors hired since 2024 are recruiting on a site that shows skydivers. The partners who lost seven colleagues without a public word have a narration problem, not a design taste problem.

Calendar

Year-end is planning season for a P.C.; the 2014 build-out ran on a hard date. Chambers submissions and Super Lawyers cycles run in Q1. A site that can carry the next ranking is worth more before it than after.

What they have shipped

2012: a real redesign after the Flash era. 2014: a new office on an aggressive timeline. 2020: fifteen alerts in a year. 2021: a template refresh. 2024 to 25: four hires. 2026: a first Chambers ranking, one memorial page done with care.

What they have not

Photographed themselves. Put a year on their proof. Claimed their Google profile. Written a sentence that says who the firm is for. Spoken in public since 2024. Each is a gap nobody has filled, not a preference. The website is the one place all six can be fixed at once.

16 Do · Don't · Openers that fit the record

Lead with the audit, not the agency.

Do
  • Send the audit deck through Anthony so it arrives as a partner's find, not a vendor's pitch.
  • Pitch Collins in the words the partners used to elect him: growing "the Firm, its client base, and markets," "exceptional value and client service," "attract top talent." Then a term sheet: what, who, how long, what happens if it slips.
  • Use their own vocabulary: "We Listen," "It's About Results," "Alternative Fee Arrangements." Those pages are the homepage they have not built.
  • Give Muehlstein the history. A timeline from 1957 with Peer's deals in it, done properly, is the veto turned into the sponsor.
  • Bring a photographer, not a mood board. The one asset they cannot download.
Don't
  • Don't quote against the other firm's number in the first meeting. Win on the case, then price.
  • Don't say template, lame or dated to anyone but Anthony. Partners approved this site twice.
  • Don't propose a five-year retainer. That is the exact shape that made Anthony write "NUTS."
  • Don't lead with technology. Nobody there has said a public word about it. Lead with the client in the elevator.
  • Don't touch the wordmark. It is the one designed thing they own.
Openers that fit the record
  • "Your About page says fifty-five years. You are sixty-nine." Cites about.html; every partner will check it on the spot.
  • "Chambers ranked you for the first time in August. It is on your third screen in 15-pixel type." Cites the Aug 2026 news item and the homepage.
  • "Burke Warren was on your exact template in January 2022 and got a new one by January 2023, from your own vendor." Cites the Wayback captures. For Muehlstein: "You cut the rent thirty percent in 2014. This costs less than a year of the difference." Cites Crain's, May 2015.
  • "Eric Kordish's page has fifteen deals with dollar figures on it. That is the best writing on your site and it is under a cookie wall." Cites the bio.

17 Four openings · labeled as the author's read

Sell the site as the firm's first public voice in a decade.

01 · The rebuild, bought the 2014 way

A fixed-scope site, a hard date, a small partner group and the COO

What: the site the audit describes, people first, dated proof, no wall, under two seconds on a phone. Why it fits: the firm's one documented purchase of this size (the office) was bought exactly this way (slide 14), and Anthony's objection is the other firm's shape, not the idea (slide 15). Frame it as a pilot with a kill criterion: the homepage and ten bios live by a date, or no second phase.

02 · The photo day

One day at 161 North Clark, twenty-six people, the room

What: a shoot that produces the index faces, the homepage, and a year of news imagery. Why it fits: no photograph of a person at the firm exists anywhere on the site in any era (audit slide 03), and it is the one thing the incumbent vendor cannot template. Sellable alone, and it makes the rebuild inevitable.

03 · Claim the record

Google profile, directories, the results page with years on it

What: claim and fix the Google Business Profile (2.8 stars, "Own this business?"), correct Suite 3100 in FindLaw and Lexology, date every matter on focus-results. Why it fits: cheap, fast, measurable, and it is where a referral looks before the site (slide 10). A first invoice that proves the firm can trust the second.

04 · Narration as a retainer, monthly not five-year

One item a month with a face on it, the newsletter form fixed

What: a monthly cadence that turns hires, rankings, deals and memorials into dated, authored items. Why it fits: the firm publishes five times a year and has no public voice (slide 12); the 2020 pace proves it can (slide 07). Month to month, cancelable, the opposite of the quote on the table.

Counterparty read: a 69-year-old P.C. with nineteen partners and no distress signals found. Normal terms. The risk is a committee that cannot decide, not a client that cannot pay. The first move is the audit deck, forwarded by Anthony, with one sentence from John.
