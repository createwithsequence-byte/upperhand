01 The read in one breath

Real members, invented faces.

Thirty-odd real chefs have paid to join a community whose public face is eleven invented ones. Everything a first-time visitor can see is a promise. Everything that would sell it, a three-star chef in San Francisco, an executive chef in Ibiza, a knife maker from Thiers, is an empty card behind a name. The site is well made and fast to build on. It is not yet telling the truth it could tell.

05 The words · read across all 31 pages

The voice is the asset. Four sentences undercut it.

Keep · the vocabulary

"The kitchen is bigger than the restaurant." "This part of the house is for members." "Without the velvet rope." "No stage voice." "Made at the pass. Shared beyond it." A chef reads this and knows it was written by one. No peer site has a voice this specific. Do not let a rebuild flatten it.

Cut · the four sentences a search can break
  • "More than 20 years as a Michelin-starred chef." The record is one star, Atelier Wilma, 2017 guide. Say that; it is still a star.
  • "Every member is verified." Eleven of the cards are written characters.
  • "Less than your daily Starbucks." On four pages. It prices the community against a coffee, not against a career.
  • "Posted by Illustrative community listing." Two example offers on the pop-up board, labeled EXAMPLE, on a page that asks members to post real ones.

Also: em dashes in nearly every paragraph, which reads as generated to anyone who reads a lot of generated text; "Texturs by MR" on the founder's own card; the events page says "The first dates are being set" on 24 and 28 September alike.

06 The paywall · Discover, Boards, The Pass, Jobs, Account

Five of eight nav items open the same wall. Nothing shows what is behind it.

  • Same page five times. /feed, /boards, /videos, /jobs and /account all render "This part of the house is for members." A first-time visitor clicks Discover and is asked for a card.
  • No preview. No sample thread, no clip of The Pass, no job listing, no session date. The pricing page promises "the full recording archive" and "long-form podcasts"; the public site shows none of either.
  • "Have a founding invite code?" sits on the wall as plain text. It is the one line that hints at a real community, and it goes nowhere for a stranger.
  • Sign-up is good. Google, Apple or email, three fields, "Chef de cuisine" as the name placeholder. Keep it. It just needs a reason to be reached.

Part three

What is bad

Six findings, each with the mechanism and the fix. Then the verdict, then what must not be touched.

15 THE BENCH · six findings, ranked by leverage · defect or preference · 1 to 3

Delete eleven people, ask twenty-four for a photo, open the door a crack.

01 · Defect · Invented members

Eleven seeded personas sit beside real ones, badged.

Mechanism: the invented cards are the complete ones, so the proof on the page is the part that fails a search. Fix: delete the eleven tonight; keep their bios as the placeholder copy real members replace.

02 · Defect · Empty real profiles

23 of 24 real members have no photo, kitchen or line.

Mechanism: sign-up stops at name, email, password; nothing asks for a picture or a kitchen, nothing nudges after. Fix: three steps after sign-up (phone photo, current kitchen, one sentence), then a personal email to the 24 this week.

03 · Defect · No look inside

Five nav items open one wall; nothing previews the product.

Mechanism: the sale asks for trust before showing anything; every peer shows first. Fix: a public "This week at the pass" page: next live table with a date, one thread with names, one clip. Plus a 7-day trial or a free tier for members' brigades.

16 THE BENCH · six findings · 4 to 6

Then the phone, the four sentences, and the badge.

04 · Defect · Phone speed

4 to 8 s to the headline on a phone.

Mechanism: Google Fonts blocking 922 ms, Stripe.js on every page, 1600 px images sent to 375 px screens. Fix: self-host the fonts with swap, Stripe at checkout only, srcset and AVIF. An afternoon; expect 85 plus.

05 · Defect · Words a search breaks

"20 years Michelin-starred," "every member is verified," a terms date set to today.

Mechanism: the first things a sceptic checks, and the record contradicts them. Fix: "Michelin-starred at Atelier Wilma, Frankfurt"; "founding members are verified by us"; a real date on terms and privacy.

06 · Preference · Badges and examples

A Lovable badge on a paid product; example listings on the board.

Mechanism: the badge covers links on phones and says "prototype"; EXAMPLE listings tell the patron the board is empty. Fix: remove the badge (a plan setting); replace the examples with two real offers, or an honest empty state with a date.

18 The keep list · what the rebuild must not destroy

Keep the name, the voice, the type, the price, and the pair.

The vocabulary

The pass, every station, the house, live tables, without the velvet rope. Already a brand. Write the app in it too.

The type and the gold

Instrument Serif over Work Sans, one gold, navy-black. Disciplined across 31 pages. Do not redesign it; photograph into it.

$9 and $89, two plans

One membership, cancel any time, one sentence of terms. Thirty people bought it before the app existed. The price is right; the proof is missing.

The founders, as a pair

A chef and a community professional who ran Yelp Frankfurt and Canva's creator community. The story is true and it is the right story. Put both of them on camera.

The pop-up board idea

"Put a room, a menu, or a pair of hands on the table." No peer has this. It only needs real listings.

The sign-up

Google, Apple, email, three fields. Fine as is; add the three profile steps after it, not before.

The build itself

Sound stack, clean markup, accessibility 100. A rebuild from zero would be a mistake; a build on this is the job.

Thirty members

Richard Lee, Mark Vaessen, Roland Lannier, Johannes Frankenfeld and twenty more real people. The asset. Everything below starts with them.

What we would change

Six moves, the app as it stands, three directions, and what a member notices. Labeled as our read. No price in this document.

19 The sweeping changes · each names the visitor it serves and the finding it answers

Make the thirty visible, show the inside, ship the app they already paid for.

01 · Real people first · the line cook

Delete the seeds. Photograph the members: a phone, window light, whites, one instruction sent to 24 people. The chefs page becomes the homepage's second screen. Answers findings 01 and 02.

02 · Show the inside · the cook, the sous chef

"This week at the pass": a dated public page with the next live table, one thread, one clip, one job. Plus a trial or a free tier for members' brigades. Answers 03.

03 · Onboarding that builds the card · the invited member

Three steps after sign-up: photo, current kitchen, one line. A reminder that says why. The empty card is not a member's fault; it is the form's.

04 · Fast on a phone · everyone

Self-hosted fonts, Stripe at checkout only, sized images. Then Sentry plus a product analytics event set, so the next audit measures behaviour, not guesses. Answers 04.

05 · Words that survive a search · the journalist, the partner

Rewrite the four sentences. Date the terms. Replace the example listings. Turn the partners wall into partners with offers, or shorten it to the ones who said yes. Answers 05 and 06.

06 · The app · the thirty

Eleven member screens already exist behind /auth. Audit them with members in the room, fix the top five, then wrap for the stores. Thirty people are paying for a product they open in a browser tab. See the next slide.

21 Three directions · named here, built as live pages in the next pass

Same words, same gold. The variable is what the pictures are of.

01 · The pass · our pick

The site as a documentary of the members' kitchens.

Photographs made by members on their own passes, window light, whites, no styling. Instrument Serif at scale over them. Roster-first: the second screen is faces. Motion: one slow drift on the hero, nothing else. Says: these people are real and they are in here. Risk: it needs 24 photographs in two weeks.

02 · The brigade

The site as the kitchen's own roster board.

A directory: name, station, kitchen, city, in a ruled table with mono labels, faces small and consistent. Swiss, dense, honest. The number of members is the headline. Says: a working list, not a brochure. Risk: cold for a $9 consumer decision; strongest for partners and press.

03 · The service

The site as this week's schedule.

The next live table, dated and hosted, is the hero; boards and jobs as today's items; a countdown. Editorial grid, serif headlines, live-red for what is on now. Says: it is happening, come in. Risk: only works once the cadence is real; punishes a quiet week.

Why the pass: the asset is people, the voice already talks about them, and it changes nothing a member has bought. The brigade is the partners page; the service is the digest. Two pages per direction, real names and photographs, come in the next pass.
