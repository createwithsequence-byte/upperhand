What we are proposing

To the partners of Pedersen & Houpt,

In the last few years the firm represented JRTC Holdings in buying the Thompson Center, advised Skolnik Industries on its sale to Pelican Energy Partners, and received its first Chambers recognition. The website mentions each of these once, if at all.

You asked us what the website is for. Our answer is in section two: it should be the firm's first meeting with a client, a way to be found, and a reason to come back. The rest of this document is how we would get it there, with launch a full year ahead of the firm's seventieth anniversary in October 2027.

It is one fixed-fee project, itemized below: a new site, photography at 161 North Clark, the copy and search work behind it, and six articles written from work the firm has already made public. Ten weeks from kickoff to launch. We would build it with you, starting from the partners' own answers about how clients find and choose the firm. 02 · Priorities & goals

What the website is for

By the partners' own account, the site brings in almost no client contact today. It should do three jobs.

  1. 01

    The first meeting

    A referral or a lateral candidate will often look at the site before calling. The homepage should put the firm's people and matters first, with no stock photography and the client login where clients expect it.

  2. 02

    Being found

    Today the firm is hard to find in search for anything but its own name. Every practice page should answer the questions clients type, and every closed matter should become a page that can rank.

  3. 03

    A reason to come back

    About five posts a year is too few for a firm this active. Regular articles with bylines and faces, six of them on launch day, and a year of them in place before the anniversary.

03 · What we'll do

Scope of work

Website design

A design system and seven templates, designed phone-first and people-first: faces on the homepage, no stock photography, and the firm's own logo and red. What it looks like comes out of discovery, from the partners' answers about the firm, its clients and its people. It comes with a clickable prototype, two rounds of revisions and a brand playbook so later edits stay consistent.

Website development

A fast, secure site on Next.js and Vercel, with a headless CMS your team can edit without calling anyone. We would recommend Sanity and confirm it in discovery. Every page, bio and archived publication moves over, every old address redirects, and LawPay, client login, the newsletter and forms carry across. We handle the move from Firmseek, including the domain, so the switch happens without downtime.

Optimization

Pages that load in under 2 seconds on a phone and meet WCAG 2.2 AA, with cookie consent built into the design rather than added at the end. Each page gets a written description and structured data. We set up Google Analytics 4, keeping your history, and Search Console, and correct the Google Business Profile and directory listings.

Content

Photography on site at 161 North Clark, and the content you already have, moved over and improved: every existing page sorted and updated, new titles and descriptions, tags by practice, clean addresses with redirects so search traction carries over, and one house style throughout. Plus every practice page rewritten around what clients search for, an edit of every bio, six new articles and a keyword map for each practice.

Designed with you

The design comes from working with you. In discovery we ask the partners dedicated questions about the firm, its clients and its people, and the site is built from their answers.

We have already sketched a few starting points from our September audit. We'll bring them to the kickoff as conversation starters.

Search

  • A written description on every page. Two of the 169 pages we crawled have one, so Google writes its own. On attorney pages it has been pulling homepage text such as "cookie settings, client focus."
  • Structured data for the firm, each attorney and each practice, so search engines can read the site the way a client would.

Keeping what already ranks

A new site should not start from zero. Before anything moves, we pull a year of search data for every page in the sitemap and sort each one. Pages that earn visits or links keep their standing: the old address redirects to the new one, page for page, so the traction carries over. Their titles and descriptions get rewritten for how clients actually search, and their copy gets updated where the law or the facts have changed.

  1. KeepMoves over with a new title and description. Bios and news, for example.
  2. UpdateCopy refreshed, dated, bylined and tagged by practice. Publications whose law has moved on.
  3. MergeOverlapping pages combined into one, every old address pointing at it. The 34 practice pages become seven.
  4. RetireNo traffic and no future, redirected to the closest live page, never a dead link. Likely some 2020 alerts, if the data agrees.

Practice pages that answer the question

A business owner selling a company searches for what happens next, not for "corporate and business counseling." Each practice page gets rewritten around those questions, with the lawyers who handle them beside the answer.

05 · Timeline

Ten weeks,
day one to launch

Five phases that overlap on purpose. Content overlaps the end of design and the start of the build.

  1. 01

    Discovery & planning

    A two-hour kickoff at 161 North Clark and thirty-minute interviews with the Managing Partner and a partner from each practice group. We ask dedicated questions: how clients find and choose the firm, what each practice wants to be known for, and which matters best show the work. We also read every page, the analytics and the directory listings. The phase ends with the sitemap, redirect map, shoot dates and success measures in writing.

    We need from you

    • One point person
    • Access to analytics, the domain and the current site's admin
    • Thirty minutes from a partner in each group

  2. 02

    Design

    The design comes from the partners' answers in discovery. It becomes a design system and seven templates, phone and desktop, in a clickable prototype, with a brand playbook. Each of the two revision rounds takes one consolidated set of comments from the firm.

    We need from you

    • One set of comments per round, within three business days
    • A yes on the design by the end of week 5

  3. 03

    Content & photography

    Two weeks of production: the photography, practice pages rewritten around client questions, the existing articles sorted, updated and retagged, the main page copy, bio edits, the six new articles and a description for every page.

    We need from you

    • Each attorney at the shoot for their ten minutes
    • Thirty minutes from each article's partner
    • Sign-off on copy within a week
  4. 04

    Development

    We build each template as it is approved, then move the content over and redirect every old address. A private staging link and a short progress note arrive every Friday.

    We need from you

    • Final copy approvals
    • Access to LawPay and the client login provider
  5. 05

    Optimize, test & launch

    Testing on every device and of every redirect, an hour of CMS training with a written guide, then launch on a weekday morning and two weeks of monitoring.

    We need from you

    • An hour for training
    • A launch date the partners agree on

  6. 06

    Beyond launch

    After week 10 · optional

    Launch leaves a full year before the firm turns seventy in October 2027, and that year is for publishing: each article is another way in from search, and the anniversary arrives on a site that has been kept current. Nothing after launch is on a retainer.

    Upkeep

    • Hosting passed through at cost, about $15 a month
    • A prepaid block of hours for updates and security patches
    • Another block only when you need one

    Content

    • Articles ordered as you want them
    • Weekly or monthly, with bylines and faces
    • From client questions and newly closed matters

06 · Investment

Fee and payment terms

$50,000, fixed.

The fee covers the seven line items here, from kickoff to two weeks after launch. It is not an estimate and it does not grow with hours. If we've underestimated the work, that is ours to absorb. The largest line is development, which covers the build, the migration and the redirects. The firm owns the site, its code and its content, and has full rights to the photographs.

Payment terms

Three payments: 40 percent at signing, 30 percent when you approve the design at the end of week 5, and 30 percent at launch. Invoices are due in fifteen days.

What would change it

Work not listed here, such as a client portal, a second office or a practice group added mid-build. We would quote it as a fixed fee before starting.

This proposal holds through October 31, 2026.

After launch

None of this is in the $50,000 and none of it is required. Nothing is on a monthly retainer: hosting passes through at cost, and everything else you buy when you need it.
