The firm is better than its website.

Brian, Anthony, and the partners,

Pedersen & Houpt has spent sixty-nine years on work that rarely makes it onto the page. Counsel to the buyer of the Thompson Center. Counsel to a Chicago steel-drum maker through its sale. A first Chambers ranking this summer. Your website carries almost none of it, and what it does carry sits on a design from 2012, refreshed once in 2021.

We're proposing one project with a start and an end. A new site. A day of photography at 161 North Clark. The copy and search work underneath it. And the first six articles, written from matters you've already closed and already made public.

Ten weeks from kickoff to launch, for one fixed price, paid in three parts tied to work you can see. Everything we say about your current site comes from our September audit of it. Where we say something is missing, we checked.

02 · Priorities & goals

What this has to do.

  1. 1

    Look like the firm.

    For most new clients and every lateral hire, the site is the first meeting. It should read like 161 North Clark, not a template. The people and the matters lead.

  2. 2

    Get found.

    When someone searches for a Chicago business or real estate firm, the site should answer with a written title, a fast page and a Google profile the firm actually controls.

  3. 3

    Stay current.

    A site is only as current as its last post. We build a publishing habit the firm can keep, starting with six articles on launch day.

03 · What we'll do

Design. Build.
Tune. Fill.

Four jobs, done by the same two people who wrote this proposal. The fourth is the one most rebuilds leave out.

Website design

Build

Website development

A fast, secure site your team edits without calling anyone. Everything on the old site moves over, and every old address keeps working.

  • A CMS for bios, matters and articles
  • Every page, bio and archived publication migrated

Tune

Optimization

Speed, search and access, measured before and after, so the improvement is a number and not an opinion.

One day at
161 North Clark.

Six partners, four backdrops. Portraits are the most-viewed images on any law firm's site, and today they don't match. One shoot day fixes that and gives the firm photography nobody else has: your office, your building, your street.

The shot list

  • Portraits. Every attorney, one lighting setup, in color, as a full frame and a cut-out.
  • The office. The floor you designed in 2014, glass walls and working rooms, in use.
  • The building and the Loop. 161 North Clark, the street, the river. Documentary, never stock.
  • Details. Hands, files, the reception desk, the things on the shelves.

On the day

  • A two-person crew arrives at 8.
  • Portraits run ten minutes each, on a schedule we publish a week ahead, so nobody waits.
  • Office and street shots fill the gaps between portraits.
  • About 150 retouched selects within five business days, with usage rights for the firm.
Reference frames, not ours. Ours come from the shoot.

The foundation underneath

  • A title and description on every page. Two of the 169 pages we crawled have one today.
  • A redirect for every old address. Links from directories, old articles and years of email signatures keep working on day one.
  • Structured data for the firm, each attorney and each practice, so search engines read the site the way a client would.
  • Google Business Profile, claimed. Today it shows 2.8 stars on four reviews and appears to be unclaimed.
  • Listings that agree with each other. FindLaw and Lexology still list Suite 3100. Your site says Suite 2700.
  • A keyword map for each practice, set in discovery and tracked monthly after launch.

05 · Timeline

Ten weeks,
day one to launch.

Five phases that overlap on purpose. Content starts while design finishes, and the build starts before content is done. Each payment lands on work you've already seen.

  1. 01

    Discovery & planning

    A two-hour kickoff at 161 North Clark, then thirty-minute interviews with the Managing Partner and each practice leader. We read every page in your sitemap, your analytics and your directory listings. You leave this phase with the plan, in writing.

    You get

    • Sitemap and content model
    • A redirect map for every old address
    • The shoot plan and a date
    • Success measures, signed off

    We need from you

    • One point person
    • Access to analytics, the domain and the current site's admin
    • Thirty minutes from each group leader

  2. 02

    Design

    We start from the Matter of record direction you saw in September and turn it into a system: type, color, and the tombstone as a component the whole site can use. Seven templates, desktop and phone, in a prototype you can click. Two rounds of revisions, with one consolidated set of comments from the firm each round.

    You get

    • The design system
    • Seven templates, desktop and phone
    • A clickable prototype

    We need from you

    • One set of comments per round, within three business days
    • A yes on the design by the end of week 5

  3. 03

    Content

    Two weeks of production, overlapping the end of design and the start of the build. The shoot day at 161 North Clark. Copy for the home, practice, about and careers pages. An edit of every bio. The six launch articles, each from a thirty-minute partner interview, plus a written title and description for every page on the site.

    You get

    • About 150 retouched photographs
    • All site copy, edited bios
    • Six approved articles
    • A keyword map

    We need from you

    • Each attorney at the shoot for their ten minutes
    • Thirty minutes from each article's partner
    • Sign-off on copy within a week
  4. 04

    Development

    We build as each template is approved. Everything moves over: bios, practices, news and the publications archive. Every old address gets a redirect. LawPay, the client login, the newsletter and the forms come across. A staging link lands in your inbox every Friday, so you watch the site come together instead of waiting for a reveal.

    You get

    • The working site on a private staging link
    • The CMS, with your content already in it
    • A weekly progress note

    We need from you

    • Final copy approvals
    • Access to LawPay and the client login provider
  5. 05

    Optimize, test & launch

    Speed, accessibility and every device. We test every redirect, train your team on the CMS in an hour with a written guide to keep, and launch on a weekday morning. Then we watch it for two weeks: search, speed, forms, anything that moves.

    You get

    • The new pedersenhoupt.com, live
    • Training and a written guide
    • Search Console and your Google profile, verified
    • Two weeks of watch after launch

    We need from you

    • An hour for training
    • A launch date the partners agree on

  6. 06

    Beyond launch

    Month three onward · optional

    A site stays current only if someone keeps it that way. After launch, two plans run month to month, and you can stop either one with thirty days' notice.

    Care plan

    • Hosting and security updates
    • Uptime monitoring and backups
    • A block of hours each month for changes

    Content program

    • A steady cadence: six articles a month
    • Drawn from inbound questions and new matters
    • Partner interview, search brief, LinkedIn cut

Sign by October 9 and kickoff is the week of October 12. Launch lands the week of December 14, before the holidays.

06 · Investment

One project.
One price.

$50,000, fixed.

It covers everything on this page, from the kickoff to two weeks after launch. It is not an estimate and it does not grow with hours. If we've underestimated the work, that is ours to absorb.

The price is itemized so you can see where it goes. The largest share is the build, because a site the firm can edit and keep fast is most of the value. The content lines are the part other proposals leave out.

Payment terms

Three payments, each tied to something you can see: 40 percent when we sign, 30 percent when you approve the design at the end of week 5, and 30 percent at launch. Invoices are due in fifteen days.
